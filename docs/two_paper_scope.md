# Two papers: claims and tests

The publication goal is an understandable physical prediction with a derivation
and a fair test. Familiar mathematics can support a new physical prediction;
it does not automatically establish or rule out that prediction's originality.
A focused comparison with the closest physical mechanisms is still needed.

## Paper 1: microscopic activity and sorting

The proposed chain is: identical microscopic activity rules, flow-dependent
coarse disturbance, residence-time differences, concentrated sharing. The hidden
layer is part of this argument. More coupled interactions can contribute more
fluctuations without imposing a different microscopic activity level. The lattice
can index abstract degrees of freedom rather than physical positions. Contact
footprints are one realization; interaction rates provide another. No particular
spatial dimension or surface-area law is required. The flow-dependent coupling
is specified in the existing construction, rather than derived from the existence
of a lattice alone.

The derived contact case assumes unbiased independent contributions in the measured
state coordinate and contact allocation proportional to actual flow. It gives
D_i proportional to J_i, exactly the q=m line of the conserved-flow model after
a common time rescaling. This is inside the theorem's window for m>=1. Uniform
microscopic activity alone supplies neither the contact-allocation law nor the
required independence and absence of drift. Existing deterministic examples
support seeding and contact scaling but do not yet join every assumption in one
bounded model.

The theorem's window is an if-and-only-if statement about a *vanishing population
fraction above a pooled half-flow threshold*, as both state range and population
grow. It is not necessary for every form of unequal sharing, nor a statement that
large channels cannot exist outside the window. Channel state must be tied to a
measured physical property before calling it geometric channel size.

Two experiments must be distinguished:

- Increase activity per contact everywhere: this multiplies all D_i by the same
  constant. The model predicts unchanged stationary sharing after relaxation.
- Change how total contact disturbance scales with channel state: this changes q
  and may cross a boundary, provided the same shared normalization still holds.

For m=1, variance growing linearly or quadratically with state lies on the two
edges. Variance is squared fluctuation size; those are not exponents of the
standard deviation. Correlations among contacts can change both q and the shared
normalization, so a coherent-contact experiment is not automatically a clean
upper-edge test.

Fit drift, conditional variance, cross-channel covariance and the flow response
on calibration data. Predict the finite-N, finite-R concentration curve on separate
observations. Use physical-time pooling and adequate equilibration. A model mismatch
at calibration is not a failed test of its predicted stationary curve. A mismatch
on held-out concentration despite independently supported assumptions is relevant
counterevidence. The full local functions, not only fitted slopes, matter near edges.

Publication assessment: a coherent theoretical paper is now possible. Before
claiming a new physical mechanism, identify its closest prior models and either
supply a matched microscopic realization or specify a credible physical assay.
Physical confirmation is not claimed in the draft. The known moment inequality
should neither be sold as new nor used to dismiss the physical question.

## Paper 2: driven flow and inherited growth

The ambition is a physical account of selection in an open, driven system. Its
current evidence consists of separate constructions, rather than one derived
sequence. The paper records the five distinctions and concentrates its mathematics
on a measurable bridge: how intake, copy cost, timing, fidelity and the lifetime
of copying ability determine establishment.

For an empty accumulator receiving power W, spending E at each attempt, retaining
a viable copying state with daughter probability p, and losing copying ability at
rate mu, the mean viable lifetime output is p/(exp(mu E/W)-1). Its critical cost
is W log(1+p)/mu. The common Poisson replacement at attempt rate W/E gives Wp/mu.
The difference can reverse an establishment prediction. These are applications
of known renewal and branching mathematics; their novelty has not been established.
The accumulator enforces receipt before spending; the Poisson comparison does not.

The physical test measures the clock and its inputs independently of lineage
outcomes, then predicts a crossing of one viable daughter per lifetime. This is
conditional on an actual copying mechanism. A stronger result must produce that
mechanism and inherited differences from local interaction rules, with material
and work accounting. Prepared rare perturbations can test reachability without
estimating a billion-year waiting time. The initial ensemble and allowed events
must be specified; preparing a capable endpoint alone does not establish its origin.

Publication assessment: the current second draft is not yet ready to claim a new
first-principles mechanism. Its clearest next result is a common physical
construction with lineage tracing and a held-out establishment prediction, or
physical data that distinguish the two clocks. A broad synthesis or perspective
would require a different, literature-led contribution. We should not pad the
paper with further unrelated simulations to substitute for this missing connection.
