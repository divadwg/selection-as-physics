# One lattice description for flow and its environment

The pole is a persistent environmental feature. A new
[binary-material construction](material_results/README.md) replaces the independent
pole mask with an evolving fine material layer. Local cohesive exchanges conserve
material and retain a prepared irregular patch; its coarse occupancy obstructs
fluid and produces a circulating wake. Controls separate spatial concentration
of material, its ability to reflect flow and its cohesion.

Real-valued coarse states do not require real-valued microscopic sites. For a
block of B binary material sites, `s=sum(b)/B`. Six occupied sites out of sixteen
give s=.375. In this construction the fluid's reflected fraction is s. This is an
explicit equal-sampling and remixing closure, not a consequence of averaging
alone. Directional fluid populations remain standard lattice-Boltzmann variables;
the experiment does not derive that entire solver from binary dynamics.

The material follows a deterministic zero-temperature exchange rule with local
attraction. A substrate absorbs momentum and energy; fluid does not erode the
material. The patch is therefore supported, though no separate mask fixes its
shape. Its persistence follows the specified relaxation dynamics. A freely
suspended aggregate would require momentum exchange and motion of the material.

This closes a limited representational gap: an environmental feature can be a
coarse expression of persistent fine states. It does not yet identify that layer
with the earlier chaotic lattice that supplied effective disturbance, demonstrate
formation of a macroscopic obstruction from spatially uniform noise, or connect
the flow-concentration theorem to physical copying and heredity. Preparing a
favourable configuration establishes a conditional possibility; the preparation
must not be relabelled as observed spontaneous origin.
