\section{From shared flow to selection}
\label{sec:thought}

Concentration is organisation across channels, not necessarily structure within
a channel. Fixed pipes can share flow unequally without forming a whirlpool;
a whirlpool can persist without a few structures dominating the total flow.
The proposed connection therefore contains five distinct questions, which need
not occur in a fixed chronological order:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{.25\linewidth}p{.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Step & Evidence and remaining distinction \\
\midrule
Flow paths form & Erosion models develop channels from a rough slope. Routing
and erosion laws are specified; geometry develops through them. \\[3pt]
Flow sorts & The theorem gives an exact pooled concentration window for its
specified dynamics. It does not establish spatial structure or persistent winners. \\[3pt]
A pattern persists & The existing lattice-Boltzmann model examines circulation
behind a supplied pole. Persistence of shape is distinct from inheritance of a
particular perturbation. \\[3pt]
A pattern reproduces & Reaction--diffusion spots can divide through local
reaction and transport laws. Division alone does not establish inherited variants. \\[3pt]
Information travels & A carrier may recreate a property elsewhere. Existing token
models assume its copying and expression; a physical carrier must supply them. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The flow-structure example uses local lattice updates, a maintained inflow and
one fixed irregularity: the pole. Directional particle populations stream to
neighbours and relax through collisions. Lattice-Boltzmann rules recover
Navier--Stokes behaviour in the appropriate hydrodynamic limit \cite{lbm};
non-binary values alone do not ensure that limit. The pole's persistence is
supplied, while the circulation is produced by the dynamics. The repaired model
shows returning tracers in its nearly steady wake, absent in the low-drive,
linear and no-pole controls. Numerical details remain in the repository.

The deterministic fine-lattice experiment addresses a source of disturbance.
Starting forty coarse routes at identical states, three fine-state seeds give
final top-three flow shares of $.615$, $.463$ and $.529$, against $.075$ initially
and in frozen controls. No random numbers are drawn after initialization.
A periodic control also concentrates, so chaos is not shown necessary.
Deterministic effective noise has established mathematical precedents
\cite{melbourne,gottwald}; this lattice's drift, correlations and stochastic
convention have not been matched to Eq.~\eqref{eq:model}. It consequently does
not test the complete two-sided window.

For a constructive step, the capability must follow from the interaction rules,
rather than an instruction to copy a detected structure. The repository retains
controlled spatial examples and numerical checks separately. Its Gray--Scott
example reproduces known spot multiplication \cite{pearson,spots}; related
chemical spot replication has been observed experimentally \cite{chemicalspots}.
These are supplied reaction networks and finite prepared perturbations, not an
estimate of spontaneous origin rates. They do not yet connect the concentration
window to heredity. Molecular templating, including peptide self-replication
\cite{peptide}, provides relevant physical precedent for a carrier simpler than
a complete genetic system; it is not a demonstrated product of these flow models.

Once a copying mechanism exists, its consequences have a precise mathematical
question. If usable work arrives at rate $\eta J$ and a copy costs $E$, the ideal
long-run copying rate is $\eta\overline J/E$, subject to losses. In a rare-lineage
branching approximation, let $T$ describe inherited-state changes, $d$ disappearance,
and $P$ viable offspring states per copying attempt. Established branching theory
\cite{branching} gives the conditional establishment threshold
\begin{equation}
 E<\eta\rho\!\left[(\operatorname{diag}d-T)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(J)P\right].
\end{equation}
The repository checks this calculation against independent event simulations.
It assumes exponential copying clocks and a fixed background while the lineage
is rare. It does not derive $T$, $d$ and $P$ from the same substrate as the
concentration model, or predict finite-population takeover. Establishing that
connection, with perturbations becoming inherited variations through the
physical dynamics, is the remaining task. Rare origins need not be simulated
at historical waiting times, but their possibility requires a physical
construction and their spread requires a separate establishment test.
