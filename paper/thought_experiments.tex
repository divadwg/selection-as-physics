\section{Thought experiments toward selection}
\label{sec:thought}

\subsection{Effective noise in a deterministic world}

The first construction, \texttt{sims/core/08}, puts each of forty coarse routes
over a block of a coupled logistic-map lattice. The lattice evolves
deterministically. A route responds to the difference between its block mean
and a slowly updated running mean, with a coupling proportional to its share.
Random numbers initialize the lattice and routes; none are drawn during their
subsequent evolution. Fixing the complete initial state fixes the trajectory.

In the retained default run, the chaotic lattice gives a final top-three flow
share of $0.62$, compared with $0.08$ for a frozen lattice. Its sampled block
means have mean lag-one autocorrelation about $0.01$, and the repeated run
reports identical summary statistics. These are demonstrations of deterministic
driving with irregular coarse fluctuations and concentrated flow, not a proof
that the coarse process is Markovian or converges to the It\^o model above.

An equal-start control removes the initial differences between coarse routes
by setting every $g_i=1$, while retaining microscopic variation in the fine
lattice. After 60,000 deterministic updates, the top-three shares are $0.615$,
$0.463$ and $0.529$ for three fine-state seeds, from $3/40=0.075$ initially.
The matched frozen controls remain at $0.075$. Fine-scale dynamics therefore
seed differentiated sharing without initial coarse-channel size differences
in these runs. The possible routes and coupling rule are specified, but the
dominant routes are not supplied as an initial pattern. This is a test of
seeding within that geometry, not a test of the full two-sided window.

The periodic control is informative: it gives an even larger top-three share,
$0.95$. The experiment therefore does not show that chaos is necessary, despite
an earlier description of that control. Low autocorrelation alone would not
establish unpredictability from all coarse information either. The contribution
of the lattice experiment is to make the absence of ongoing random input
explicit and reproducible within a flow-sharing example.

Deterministic dynamics admitting stochastic effective descriptions are already
established mathematically \cite{melbourne,gottwald}. Their role here is as a
candidate physical source for the disturbance, not a new discovery that
determinism and statistical noise can coexist. A diffusion limit for this
particular lattice, including its effective drift and stochastic convention,
remains to be derived. This matters because deterministic multiplicative
forcing need not produce a driftless It\^o limit \cite{gottwald}.

\subsection{Contact geometry and the window are separate tests}

The next construction, \texttt{sims/core/09}, recruits more lattice patches for
a route with a larger share, keeping coupling per patch fixed. In the retained
run, the variance-versus-mean-share slope is $1.01$ for sums over separate
patches. A common coherent patch gives slope $2.00$; using fixed patches with
amplitude proportional to share gives $1.99$. These are across-route diagnostic
slopes, not direct estimates of conditional infinitesimal diffusivity.

The final top-three shares are $0.17$, $0.08$ and $0.63$, respectively.
The coherent case uses the same fluctuation for all routes and so changes
cross-route correlation as well as variance scaling. It cannot isolate the
effect of moving $q$ to the upper edge. Both slopes near two are also at the
$m=1$ boundary, rather than beyond it. The lattice examples consequently
support a mechanism for effective disturbance and its scaling; they do not
demonstrate the full two-sided window in a single deterministic substrate.

The exact theorem answers a different, controlled question: for the specified
diffusion, when does pooled concentration vanish as a population fraction?
The lattice routes have a lower clipping rule and no fixed upper reflecting
cutoff, and their effective drift and shared clock have not been matched to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:model}. A next test must connect those models before treating
their results as one experiment.

\subsection{From unequal sharing to differential reproduction}

Concentration alone contains no lineage. Suppose each route also has a store
of usable work $A_i$, increasing at rate $\dot A_i=\eta J_i$, and a copy costs
$E_{\rm copy}$. With constant flow and no intervening loss, the time between
copies is $E_{\rm copy}/(\eta J_i)$. For time-varying flow, accumulated work
sets the number of completed copies, up to the remainder in the store. Thus
the ideal long-run rate is $b_i=\eta\overline J_i/E_{\rm copy}$.
This derives the rate conditional on a copying mechanism, an efficiency and a
cost; it does not derive the existence of copying machinery.

In \texttt{sims/core/18}, a funded copy replaces a randomly chosen route with
an inherited state plus a perturbation. The default run with copy cost $200$
ends at mean state $8.67\pm1.27$, compared with $4.32\pm1.17$ at cost $500$
and $1.05\pm0.19$ without copying. These are means and population standard
deviations across three runs, not confidence intervals. Fitted copying-rate
slopes are $0.0045$ and $0.0017$, against ideal values $0.0050$ and $0.0020$;
replacement can erase stored resources, and self-targeted attempts are counted.

Here the connection to selection becomes explicit: state varies, is inherited,
and affects reproduction through throughput. The simulation supplies
inheritance and replacement rules and uses random draws during evolution.
It is therefore a separate thought experiment, not a completed demonstration
of selection driven entirely by the deterministic lattice. Other repository
models investigate whether copying refreshes memory faster than restoring
dynamics erase it. Those address another necessary condition for the proposed
sequence, rather than following automatically from the concentration theorem.

\subsection{Portable state as the next test}

The next priority is reproduction with heredity: a transferred state must
recreate a flow-affecting property in a new site, and that property must affect
its descendants' reproduction. The channel variable $g$ currently summarizes
behaviour; copying that number in software assumes transmission. A physical
extension should distinguish a carrier state $h$ from the channel state $g$
that develops through its interaction with local material and resources.

A candidate carrier could be a small catalytic structure or a reproducible
chemical composition. Its arrangement would hold information in the operational
sense that changing the arrangement changes what develops at the receiving
site. It need not encode an entire channel or use DNA. The same structure could
both carry the state and catalyse its expression; separation from the large
flow path need not imply separate genetic and catalytic machinery.
Schr\"odinger's aperiodic crystal supplies a historical image for a stable
arrangement with non-repeating informational content \cite{schrodinger}, but
naming such a carrier does not specify how it is copied. Experiments on
autocatalytic RNA networks illustrate why reproduction and persistence of
inherited composition require separate checks \cite{ameta}. Synthetic
self-replicators selected for photocatalytic function provide a closer chemical
comparison \cite{photocatalysis}; neither study tests this conserved-flow window.

The fine lattice supplies a candidate source of variation in this extension.
Its disturbances count as mutations only when they alter a transmissible state
and the alteration survives into descendants. Temporary fluctuations of $g$
need not do so. Copying fidelity, stability during transport and the coupling
from fine dynamics to $h$ therefore need explicit rules.

A minimal test would transfer $h$ into a fresh site, reset local $g$ and stored
work, and ask whether the carrier recreates its characteristic response under
matched conditions. Copies must consume accounted resources. Repeated transfers
would then measure whether inherited differences predict successful descendant
numbers. Scrambling the transmitted state while retaining its amount and cost
would control for material transfer alone; equalizing reproduction opportunities
would control for flow-funded selection. A failed run would constrain the tested
carrier, fidelity and timescales, not disprove the broader route. This portable
carrier construction is proposed here, not yet implemented or demonstrated.

\subsection{What the combined proposal commits to}

The scientific proposal is the connection between these steps, including the
bounded concentration condition. The theorem constrains this concentration
mechanism, not every possible route to Darwinian selection. It would gain substance from a construction
in which the same physical rules supply effective variation, cross both noise
boundaries, preserve inherited differences and fund reproduction. The
equal-start lattice control demonstrates a seed mechanism for differentiation
among initially equivalent possible routes. Extending that mechanism to a
material whose spatial flow paths and connectivity also develop requires a
spatial model of that response. What remains incomplete is the joining of
seeding, the controlled window test, memory and reproduction in one construction.
Nor does the prior art cited for the separate ingredients establish that
the complete proposed mechanism is already known.

The default outputs and equal-start controls are retained with source hashes
and reproduction commands in \texttt{docs/research\_examples/}. They report
finite-time toy-model behaviour. They neither establish empirical validity nor
replace the stationary checks below.
